# The live station: the design

Status: a design, approved by Tom on 2026-11-26 (see Decided); all eight steps of K are built (the clock, timers and transmit handshake; streaming detection; reception; busy and channel access; sending and the simulated channel; reports; identification; the hostile channel, as described under "Step 3 as built" to "Step 8 as built" below). Section F carries a correction from step 4, marked in place. Every name marked "proposed" is a suggestion, not an existing API. It designs what this library lacks: something that takes received audio as it arrives, finds and decodes bursts, decides when the channel is free, decides what to send and when, and talks to a host program. The protocol is [adaptation-protocol.md](adaptation-protocol.md). The operations and events follow the [host interface](https://github.com/packet-net/pdn-soundmodem/blob/main/docs/dev/ofdm-fm/host-interface.md) in packet-net/pdn-soundmodem. Link addresses, learning and sending by address (step one of docs/station-numbers.md) were added on 27 November 2026 and replace names and `Bind`: see "Link addresses as built" below. The sections on steps 3 to 7 describe what was built then; where link addresses changed a sentence it is marked.

## A. One clock: the receive stream's sample count

The station never reads a wall clock. Time is samples received so far at 48 kHz, and every timer is measured on it: the first slot, the random delay, how long an asked-for report may wait, the 30 s and ten-minute rules inside `LinkAdaptation` (it takes `now` in seconds; the station passes samples over 48000), and the identification interval.

A transmission is a request: "these samples, starting at receive-sample N or as soon after as possible". The audio layer reports the receive-sample index at which it started and ended. Receive audio keeps flowing, muted or not, while the radio transmits, so the count never stops. If the audio layer drops samples it says how many (proposed `Skip(count)`).

Tests push blocks of samples and are deterministic. Random numbers come from a seeded source the caller supplies.

## B. Shape of the API

A proposed `Station`, one per radio channel. Settings: the random source and whether jobs run inline; nothing else. There is no station number setting: the station picks its own when the host gives it its identity (I; "Station numbers as built"). The callsign is not a setting: the host gives it with `SetIdentity(callsign)` (H), and until it has, the station only listens.

- `Push(ReadOnlySpan<float>)`: received audio, any block size, returns quickly (D).
- `Skip(int)`, `TransmitStarted(id, sampleIndex)`, `TransmitEnded(id, sampleIndex)`, `SetExternalBusy(bool)`: from the audio layer (E).
- `SetIdentity(callsign)`: the transmitter identifier callsign, from the host (H). `Send(datagram, to, from, tag, reportWanted, priority)`, `Cancel(tag)`, `Cancel(address)`, `Snapshot()`: from the host, as the host interface defines them (`to` and `from` are link addresses: see "Link addresses as built"). Send is refused for a datagram larger than one burst to that address now carries, or a full queue.
- Out to the audio layer: events `TransmitRequest` (id, samples, earliest receive-sample) and `WithdrawRequest` (id), for a request not yet keyed when the channel turns busy.
- Out to the host, all raised by the library: `Transmitted` (on `TransmitEnded`), `Dropped`, `Received`, `PathReport`, `BurstNotDecoded`, `LinkState`, `ChannelBusy`, `Congestion`, `ModemSent`. Added, proposed: `IdentificationDue` (H).

## C. Streaming burst detection

`BurstFinder.Find` correlates a whole recording by FFT, takes its threshold from the median of the whole recording's correlation, and keeps peaks that are the largest within a symbol either side. The streaming form, for each of the three openings:

1. **Correlation in overlapping blocks.** The audio is transformed once per block and shared. Each opening has a precomputed reference spectrum (1, 3 or 6 symbols: 2112, 6336, 12672 samples). The energy under the reference comes from a running sum of squares, started afresh in every block so that it cannot drift, so the normalised value is the one `Find` computes. Built in step 2 as `StreamingBurstDetector` (internal): blocks of 32768 samples overlapping by 12671, so 20097 new values per opening per block; one forward transform and two inverse ones per block (two openings share an inverse, one in its real part and one in its imaginary part).
2. **Threshold:** the larger of 0.1 and 7 times a running median of the correlation (every 16th value, as `Find` does) over the last 30 s, kept as a histogram. The estimate pauses over each burst whose header has been read, from its first sample to its last (by sample index, as step 3 does it), and during the station's own transmissions, so a strong burst does not lift it. For the first 5 s only 0.1 applies, and the station does not transmit.
3. **The same peak rule, one symbol late.** The offline rule is not quite "the largest within a symbol either side": it keeps strict local maxima over the threshold, then works from the highest down and removes any peak closer than a symbol to one it has kept. So a lower peak beside a higher one that was itself removed stays. The streaming form applies that rule to a short list of pending peaks and settles a peak once every higher peak within a symbol is settled and a symbol past it has been seen. A candidate comes out between 0.24 and 0.46 s after its reference ends in the vectors, most of it the block wait.
4. **Header read** (step 3 reads them one at a time, in the order the detector gave the candidates, each once the opening's samples are in the ring as of the last frame's end) when the opening's samples are in: 4224 samples on the full band, up to 19008 (0.4 s) on the 1.5 kHz opening. `HeaderReader.TryRead` gates the candidate. Candidates of different openings reading the same header within a symbol are one burst. `PayloadReceiver.SamplesNeeded(header)` then gives the burst's length.

Buffering: a ring of about 16 s. The longest burst is 261 symbols, 11.5 s, plus lead-in and a pad either side for the resampler. A complete burst is copied to a pooled buffer for its job, so the ring keeps moving.

Cost per second of audio, in existing pieces: one forward and two inverse transforms per block, a running sum, the histogram, and a header read per candidate. Measured in step 2: about 11 ms of CPU per second of audio for all three openings (an i7-13700, Release), 70 percent of it in the transforms. False candidates are the risk: on white noise the full-band opening gives about 300 an hour, on noise rising as an FM receiver's does none in 20 minutes, and for the first 5 s, on the floor alone, noise in the band gives many. A station is deaf to a second burst for up to 0.4 s after one starts.

## D. Decoding without blocking the audio

`Push` does correlation and peak rule only. Header reads and payload receives are jobs.

- The station queues jobs. The caller runs them on threads of its choosing: `HasWork`, `RunPending()`, `RunOne()` (built as proposed, with a `WorkAvailable` event so that a worker can sleep on a signal). Each thread running a job has its own `HeaderReader` and `TrackedReceiver` (large buffers, not thread-safe), which the station makes on first use. An inline mode, chosen at construction, runs jobs inside `Push` for tests.
- A burst arriving mid-decode is queued first in, first out, its audio already copied. More than four queued bursts: the oldest is dropped. Built: nothing is raised for it (see "Step 3 as built").
- Results are applied in order of burst start, whichever job finishes first, and `LinkAdaptation` is given the burst's end time on the sample clock, not the verdict's. Decode speed never changes what the rules see (what holds a result back until it can be applied is under "Step 3 as built").
- The channel is busy from the confirmed candidate, so a long decode does not make it look free.

A late verdict: the protocol says a failed burst is reported at once. The station sends the lone report as soon as the verdict is in and the channel has been clear 50 ms, even if the first slot passed; the prototype did the same, and nothing expires it. A newer verdict for the same station replaces it. A "drop" or "asked" report is cancelled if a data burst to that station, carrying the recommendation in its prelude, goes first. A "failed" report always goes alone, which `BurstNotDecoded` relies on (G).

## E. Busy detection

The prototype's method (the receiver's noise going quiet) fails at about -120 dBm. Busy is the union of:

- **A confirmed candidate.** If its header reads, busy to the burst's end from `SamplesNeeded`. If not, busy for the opening plus one second, then re-judged: a real burst may go on; a false candidate costs one second.
- **An unknown version.** The protocol says busy while signal is heard. Without an external indication: one second at a time while candidates keep arriving. An unknown type under a known version still carries its length.
- **The station's own transmission**, from granting a request to `TransmitEnded` plus a short tail.
- **An external indication** from the audio layer if it has one (squelch, signal strength): busy while set, and 100 ms after.

Each ends at the burst's last sample, at `TransmitEnded`, or when the indication drops. `ChannelBusy` is raised on each change. Candidates starting from a pad before the station's own transmission to 0.25 s after it are ignored: the audio is muted and a normalised correlation finds bursts in nothing. **Correction (step 6):** the 0.25 s was too long, see "Step 6 as built"; it is the own tail, 100 ms.

## F. Channel access

Terms are the host interface's: the physical state machine, T101 (priority window), T102 (slot delay), T103 (TXDELAY, the audio layer's).

- **Expedited datagrams and lone reports** go once the channel has been clear 50 ms after a burst, in the first slot.
- **Ordinary datagrams** wait for the channel to be clear for T101 plus a random T102, drawn afresh each time the channel was last busy. Proposed start: T101 0.8 s, T102 up to 0.4 s, the prototype's draw of 0.8 to 1.2 s.
- Never transmit while busy; a request not yet keyed is withdrawn if the channel turns busy.

**Correction (step 4, packet-net/pdn-soundmodem#892).** One random delay of up to 0.4 s after the first slot separates two waiting stations only if the later one can notice the earlier one's carrier within that delay, and by burst detection alone it cannot (it takes up to 0.9 s). Ordinary traffic therefore draws a whole number of slots, where a slot is the station's own time to notice a carrier, and the delay is not continuous. See "Step 4 as built" for the figures and the collision rates measured.

The prototype's rules, classified:

| Rule | Value | Class |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Clear 0.8 to 1.2 s before data, fresh draw | as stated | Convenience; kept as T101 and T102 |
| After a lone report, the reporter goes on with data; the other leaves the channel clear about 1 s | 1 s | Protocol; kept |
| Wait for the far end after a data burst | 2 s + 0.16 s per symbol | Prototype, sized to a slow decode; replaced (see Choices) |
| Hold-off after a wait that ended in silence | up to 4 s | Convenience; dropped, the random delay covers it |
| A report asked for waits for data to ride on | 1.5 s | Prototype; kept fixed (the protocol says "once the slot has passed") |
| Duty limit | 27 s in 60 s | Prototype safeguard; the audio layer's |
| Burst filled to 2.5 s, never over 9.5 s | | Made-up host and bench rule; see Choices |
| Listen 3 s first; no transmit while decoding | 3 s | Prototype; replaced by the start-up in C and by busy detection |
| Datagram size 240, 16-byte probe at first contact | | Made-up host; not needed |

After a burst with "report wanted", the sender counts a silence if nothing is heard from that station within 3 s of the burst's end (see Choices).

## G. Driving the adaptation rules

- Verdict in: `HeardData(sender, dest, heardFormat, decoded, quality, now, footing, sudden, theirReport, capability)`. Quality from `TrackedReceiver.Quality`, or `PayloadReceiver.MeasureOpening` for a failed burst; footing from `LinkAdaptation.Footing`. A returned report owed to this station is a lone report: "failed" if `Failed`, else "drop". The station adds "asked": decoded, prelude says wanted, `ReportFor` has a value. The rules never read that flag.
- A lone report for this station: `HeardReport`. If it says failed, `BurstNotDecoded` is raised with the tags of the datagrams in this station's latest burst to that sender that ended before the report began. The station knows its own transmissions, so the attribution is exact.
- Sending: `Choose(dest, now)` at the moment the station decides to go, `Sent(dest)` on `TransmitStarted`, `NoAnswer(dest)` when the answer wait runs out. The prelude carries `ReportFor`.
- `PathReport` comes from `HeardReport` and from a prelude with a recommendation; `LinkState` from `TryGetNeighbour` after each call, with "gone" after the library's ten-minute forgetting.

## H. Identification

The callsign the station identifies with is given by the host, once, with `SetIdentity`; the station does not transmit until it has one (see "Step 7 as built", "No identity"), and the same call gives it its number and id (I). The number is not a setting. On the sample clock, an identification is due when a transmission of the station's ends and either it has never identified or ten minutes (the interval of the existing Morse ident) have passed since its last identification started. Nothing else makes one due: there is no timer, and an idle station never keys to identify (the rule as decided and built is under "Step 7 as built"; earlier forms counted from the first transmission, then added a closing identification for a station that had gone quiet, which was removed on 27 November 2026). It does not look for the host identifying.

Two gaps, both closed in step 7: `BurstRenderer.RenderData` accepted only data headers, and the protocol gave the ident payload no byte layout (see "Step 7 as built").

The Morse ident is the audio layer's job in the program that hosts the modem. The station raises `IdentificationDue` on the same clock and exposes nothing else for it.

## I. Station numbers

A station picks its own number, 1 to 63, when the host gives it its identity, and makes up a 32-bit station id with it; the modems settle a number held by two stations among themselves (docs/station-numbers.md has the design; "Station numbers as built" below has the build). No host sees a number or an id: the host deals in link addresses, and the station learns where each lives. `Bind` and the names it gave to numbers are gone, and so is the constructor's number.

## J. What stays out of the library

- Sound cards and their buffers.
- Keying the transmitter and key-up timing (T103).
- Sample-rate conversion.
- The Morse ident's audio.
- The host program's framing (KISS, sockets, AX.25).
- Transmitter duty limits.

## K. Order of building

1. **Clock and plumbing:** sample clock, seeded source, event types, `Skip`, the transmit handshake with a fake audio layer. Proves timers fire at exact sample counts and one seed gives one run.
2. **Streaming detection.** Proves that, at a fixed threshold, candidates equal `BurstFinder.Find` on the same recording at block sizes of 1, 480 and 100000; start-up behaviour; a measured false-candidate rate on noise; a strong burst does not hide a weak one after it. Measures per-second cost.
3. **Reception (built):** header read, ring, job queue (inline for tests), `TrackedReceiver`, `Received`, the `LinkAdaptation` calls. Proves rendered bursts resampled by a few hundred ppm decode at every opening, and results apply in order with two workers.
4. **Busy and channel access (built):** busy union, T101 and T102, request and withdrawal. Proves the station never keys over a burst in progress and waits are exact.
5. **Sending and the simulated channel:** queue, packing, `Choose`, rendering, `Transmitted`; a test channel with delay, noise, path loss and a sample-clock offset. **First step where two stations in one process exchange datagrams**, at first-contact rates.
6. **Reports (built):** owed and lone reports, `BurstNotDecoded`, silences. Proves two stations settle on a rate, a 10 dB drop costs one burst, and a failure is reported at once when the verdict lands after the first slot.
7. **Identification (built)**, with renderer and payload layout: the burst, the clock, and learning the other stations' callsigns.
8. **Hostile channel (built):** three stations, capture effect, interference part-way through a burst, a path cut and restored.

## Step 3 as built

Where what was built differs from, or adds to, what the sections above say.

- **Candidates and header reads.** The station feeds the detector in pieces that end at each frame's end, takes the frame's candidates (sorted by index, so that a burst's own start is read before the candidates other openings find inside it), and reads headers **one at a time**, in that order, each only once its opening is in the ring as of the last frame's end. That makes "this candidate lies inside a burst already known, so ignore it" a decision taken with every earlier candidate settled: it is the same whatever the block size and however fast the workers are, and a strong burst's payload costs no reads (the test counts them). A candidate more than a symbol after a known burst's start and before its last sample is ignored unread; one nearer the start is read, and if it reads the same header as a burst already known, within a symbol, it is that burst. A data burst read under an opening other than its format's is not taken for a burst.
- **Pausing the estimate** is by sample index from a header read, to the burst's last sample, not "from a confirmed candidate": the audio of a candidate's own frame has been correlated before the candidate is out. A burst that begins inside one already marked is clipped to begin where that mark ends, since marks must not go backwards.
- **What is not strictly a function of the audio.** Whether a header read has been taken in before the detector correlates the next frame decides whether that frame's values go into the estimate. Inline, and for workers that keep up with the sample clock (a read takes 1 to 2 ms and a frame comes every 0.42 s of audio), it always has; a worker thread that is starved for a frame's worth of audio lets that frame's values into the estimate. The estimate is a 30 s median, so this moves the threshold by a hair and changes a result only if a candidate sits between the two thresholds. Nothing else the station does depends on timing: events and adaptation state do not.
- **Header reads wait for a free worker.** A read takes 1 to 2 ms and the receive of a dense burst took two or three times the burst's own length on one worker (a 2.4 s burst of 8 bits a carrier took about 8 s on a 12-thread desktop; after the tracked receiver stopped repeating work whose output was thrown away, about 1 s, 0.4 of its length: `SM_BENCH=1` in the test project prints the figure for the machine at hand), and `RunOne` takes a waiting header read before a waiting receive but cannot interrupt a receive that has begun. A host with one worker therefore sees the next burst's header read wait behind the receive in progress; nothing is lost while the ring (16.4 s) holds the audio, but the results behind it wait and the pause of the estimate is late. A host should run at least two workers, so that one is free for headers while the others receive.
- **Results** are applied when the burst's job is done and no earlier burst can still turn up: nothing unread or unreleased with an earlier index, and the detector's `CandidateFloor` (the index below which it can give no more candidates) past the burst's start. For a long burst that is long satisfied; for a report it holds the result back by up to about one frame (0.4 s) after the report ends. `Push`, `Skip` and `DeliverResults` apply what is ready, on the audio thread.
- **A burst dropped from a full queue** raises nothing. `Dropped` is the host interface's event for a datagram this station sent and tags it with the host's tag; a burst received has no tag, and the station does not know whether it was for it. It is counted (internal for now) and its place in the order is passed. Step 4's busy detection and step 8's hostile channel may want a visible figure for it.
- **"Asked" reports** (the prelude says "report wanted", the burst decoded, `ReportFor` has a value) were not recorded as owed in step 3 (recorded and sent in step 6): `ReportFor` marks the neighbour as told, and that belongs with sending, in step 6. What the rules return from `HeardData` (a report "failed" or "drop") is recorded as owed, one for each neighbour, the newest replacing the one before it, with the sample the burst ended at.
- **`LinkState`** is raised after each call on the rules for a neighbour when its format (what `Choose` gives now), the room in a burst at that format or where the format came from changes, and with `Gone` set when ten minutes on the sample clock pass without a word from it. The room is the longest burst's payload less the largest prelude and the datagram's own length: the largest datagram that goes whole, as Choice 5 has it. Where nothing is yet known the format and rate are null.
- **`ReceivedEvent`'s quality** is the mean over the payload's carriers of the measured quality in dB, before the footing is taken off. The datagram's memory is valid only during the event.
- **Names.** (Superseded on 27 November 2026: events are by link address and `Bind` and the placeholders are gone; see "Link addresses as built".) A station heard by number alone was named by the ASCII text "#" and the number in decimal in `PathReport` and `LinkState`, and `Received.Sender` was null.
- **A lone report for another station, or for everyone,** is read, takes its place in the order, and is neither measured nor acted on.
- **Skip** feeds silence through the ring and the detector, so a gap in the audio is a gap in the ring too.

## Step 4 as built

Where what was built differs from, or adds to, what sections E and F say. The rules are in `ChannelAccess` (a clock, a seeded source and a few callbacks, so that two of them can be run on one test clock); `Station` joins them to its clock, receive path and audio-layer handshake.

- **Busy.** The union of: a known burst, from the frame that read its header to its last sample; a candidate that will not read, from the candidate's first sample for its opening and one second; an unknown version, one second from the read, again for each header that reads; a candidate waiting to be judged; the station's own transmission, from the grant to `TransmitEnded` plus 100 ms; the host's indication (`SetExternalBusy`), while set and for 100 ms after. Every end is a sample index and a timer on the sample clock, so `ChannelBusy(false)` is raised with the clock at exactly that sample, whatever the block size. `ChannelBusy` is raised on a change of the union only. A burst already over when its header is read (a lone report is found 0.24 to 0.46 s after it ends) shows as busy for the moment it was being judged and clear again at once; the wait that follows is counted from the burst's last sample.
- **Candidates that are the same burst.** A burst is found under each opening whose reference it resembles, and the readings under the wrong openings fail. Left alone, each would hold the channel for a second after every short burst, and each would wait a frame for its opening. A candidate inside a known burst or within a symbol of its start is therefore dropped unread (it is counted with those ignored), is not waited for, and a failed read within a symbol of a known burst's start adds no hold. The holds of failed reads are applied once no candidate is left to judge, so that the order the readings come in does not matter. An unknown type under a known version holds the channel for the length its header carries.
- **The station's own transmission.** From a symbol before the audio layer's reported start to 0.25 s after its reported end, candidates are ignored (counted in `CandidatesMuted`), and the detector's threshold estimate is paused over the transmission and the 0.25 s.
- **The start.** Nothing is granted in the first 5 s, the time the detector's threshold floor alone applies (section C).
- **The time to notice a carrier** is 100 ms (4800 samples) once the host has called `SetExternalBusy` at all, set or clear; before that, the detection latency of the slowest opening: the renderer's lead-in (4224), the later of the reference plus a symbol and the opening the header read needs (19008 on the 1.5 kHz opening), and a frame's new values (20097): 43329 samples, 0.903 s. The lead-in is in it because a station's request starts before the first training sample, and a slot is the gap between two stations' starts. A slot at least this long means a station one slot behind another always hears it first.
- **Ordinary traffic** waits for the channel to be clear for 0.8 s and then a whole number of slots drawn from the seeded source (`Random.Next(window)`) the first time the wait is judged after the channel was last busy. The window is a fixed constant for each case: **4 slots of 100 ms** with a carrier indication (0 to 0.3 s, as near the design's 0.4 s of spread as whole slots get), **2 slots of 0.9 s** with detection alone (0 or 0.9 s: the same count would cost up to 2.7 s of dead air and 1.35 s on average). `ChannelAccess.SlotWindow()` is the seam for packet-net/pdn-soundmodem#892, where the window will follow the stations heard and the collisions seen. If the indication appears mid-wait the draw is kept and the slot shortens (the wait is recomputed, and the request goes at once if the new time has passed); if the channel turns busy first the draw is dropped and made again.
- **Reports and expedited datagrams** go 50 ms after the channel was last busy. **After a lone report for this station** (applied when the report's result is, up to a frame after it ends), ordinary traffic may not be granted before a second after the report's last sample; an ordinary request already handed over and not keyed is taken back. Reports for another station or for everyone hold nothing.
- **Order.** Report (and the identification owed), then expedited, then ordinary, first in first out within a class. One request is handed to the audio layer at a time; the next waits for its end, the 100 ms tail and its own class's wait.
- **Withdrawal.** A granted, not yet keyed request goes back (`WithdrawRequest`) and is queued again, in its place in its class, when any cause other than the station's own turns the channel busy. A request the audio layer started anyway (the race the handshake already tolerates) is on air, is not asked for again, and a second hand-over of it is withdrawn.
- **The entry point** is `Station.Enqueue(samples, class)` (internal; step 5 calls it), returning an id, with an internal event `AccessChanged` for granted, withdrawn, started and ended. `RequestTransmit` and `WithdrawTransmit` remain as the handshake below it.
- **Threads.** `SetExternalBusy` and `Enqueue` are audio-thread members, serialised with `Push`, and, like `TransmitStarted` and `TransmitEnded`, may not be called from an event handler or timer action. They raise events on the calling thread. No worker member changes.
- **What the correction measured** (two stations, a third station's burst ending at the same sample for both, each with an ordinary datagram waiting, 4000 rounds, the other's transmission noticed at the first frame boundary after its opening is in, the opening drawn at random, each station's frame phase its own; with an indication, it follows the carrier within 100 ms): with detection alone the original single delay (0 to 0.4 s) had both on air at once in 98.2 percent of rounds, the slotted draw in 48.8 percent. With a carrier indication, 23.2 and 25.9 percent. The slotted figures are the chance of two stations drawing the same slot, one in the window, as theory says: a station one slot behind always hears the other first. The slots cost something where a continuous delay is already enough (with an indication the slotted draw collides a little more often, not less), and the window is the lever #892 will move.

## Step 5 as built

The host's operations and the bursts that carry what it sends (`Station.Send.cs`), and a simulated channel in the test project (`SimChannel.cs`, `SimRig.cs`). Where the design left room, or the build differs:

- **Threads.** `Send`, `Cancel` (by tag and by address) and `Snapshot` are audio-thread members, like `Push` and `SetExternalBusy`: the host serialises them with `Push` (one thread or a lock), and they throw from an event handler. They raise `TransmitRequest`, `Dropped` and `Congestion` on the calling thread.
- **`Bind(name, number)`.** (Removed on 27 November 2026: see "Link addresses as built".) A name has one number and a number one name; binding a name that is bound elsewhere moves it, and the old number goes back to its placeholder. Names that are empty, `LinkAddress.Everyone` (new: the reserved name for broadcasts) or a placeholder ("#7") are refused, as is a number outside 1 to 63 or the station's own. A station bound after it was heard is announced once more in a `LinkState` event under its new name; events already raised keep the placeholder; later events (`Received.Sender`, `PathReport`, `LinkState`, `Transmitted`) carry the name. Bindings survive the ten-minute forgetting.
- **`Send`.** Returns `SendResult`: `Accepted`, `TooLarge` (the datagram is larger than the longest burst, 252 symbols, at the format the station would use now, less the prelude's largest size of 4 bytes and the datagram's length of 2: 342 bytes at first contact, the figure `LinkState.DatagramRoom` gave; for everyone no prelude, 346; link addresses changed these figures, see "Link addresses as built") and `QueueFull`; `NotBound` is gone. The bytes are copied into a buffer from the station's own pool, so a steady flow allocates nothing (the test shows zero bytes a burst once a shape has been seen; the first burst of a shape costs the renderer's buffers, about 8 MB). A datagram accepted when the link was faster can be too large for the format after it falls: it is dropped with the new `DropReason.NoLongerFits`. `Expired` and `LinkForgotten` are not raised (a forgotten station is sent to again from first contact; a queue is bounded and the host cancels).
- **Queues.** One per destination (since 27 November 2026, one per link address), two first-in-first-out lists (expedited ahead of ordinary). The bound is **30 s of airtime at the format in use** (`QueueLimitSeconds`): at least two of the longest burst (11.5 s) so that the channel is not left idle at the bottom of the ladder, and about what a link layer's retry timer waits at that rate. The queue is full when an accepted datagram takes it to the limit (it can exceed it by one datagram), `Congestion(writable: false)` is raised, and `Send` is refused until it falls below half (`QueueResumeFraction`), when `Congestion(writable: true)` is raised. An empty queue accepts any datagram that fits one burst.
- **The data request.** While anything is queued the station keeps one request with the channel-access rules (a reused `ChannelAccess.Request`), in the class of the most urgent datagram; it is reclassified while it waits unbuilt, so that an expedited datagram sent after an ordinary one takes the first slot. `ChannelAccess` gained `Prepare` (called at the grant, before the hand-over, to decide what goes; false means there is nothing to send after all), `Remove`, `Reclassify`, and an `Enqueue` that reuses a request.
- **Building a burst, at the grant.** Choose the destination, then `Choose(dest, now)`, then take datagrams in order (expedited first) while the payload fits the airtime ceiling of **2.5 s including lead-in and opening** (`BurstCeilingSamples`; 44 payload symbols at the first-contact format, 57 bytes), the first always (a single datagram that needs longer goes alone and whole). The prelude carries `ReportFor(dest)`, "report wanted" when `Choose` says so or any datagram in the burst asked, and the capability word (`CapabilityWord`, 0: revision 0, no flags) whenever "report wanted" is set; four bytes of prelude are reserved either way. **A burst for everyone** has no prelude and no report and goes at the **first-contact format**: the protocol says nothing about the format of broadcasts, and the most robust one reaches the furthest. The burst number counts up per data burst built, modulo 16, per station.
- **Which destination next** (fair rule): among destinations with something queued of the granted class (an expedited grant takes only those with expedited datagrams), the one served longest ago; the lowest number (everyone first) on a tie. (Now by address, the one sent to first on a tie.)
- **Bookkeeping.** `Sent(dest)` at `TransmitStarted` (never for everyone); `Transmitted` for each datagram at `TransmitEnded`, with the airtime measured from the reported start to the reported end (lead-in included), the format's rate in bit/s and the clock at the end. `ModemSent` is wired (a request enqueued with a `ModemSentEvent` raises it at its start) and raised for nothing yet.
- **Withdrawal.** The design says there is nothing to undo, and that holds, with one rule: **a burst, once built, is kept**. `Choose` changes nothing; `ReportFor` marks the neighbour as told, and the same prelude goes when the burst is handed over again; `Sent` is recorded only at the start. A withdrawn request stays in the queue with its burst (same datagrams, format, prelude, burst number and audio) and is handed over again unchanged when the channel allows. This also keeps the tolerated race (the audio layer keys a request already withdrawn) exact: what it plays is what the station thinks it sent, and nothing is sent twice. The costs: the format was chosen up to a few seconds before it goes, and the datagrams of a withdrawn burst can no longer be cancelled. The simulated channel shows it (a request granted, withdrawn when the other station's burst was heard, handed over again with the same checksum and burst number, delivered once).
- **The answer wait and `NoAnswer`.** After a burst that asked for a report, if no header of any burst from that station is read between the burst's end and the wait's end, `NoAnswer(dest)` is called. **The wait is 3 s plus twice the burst's airtime** (opening and payload, lead-in not counted), from the burst's end (`Station.AnswerWaitSamples`), not the design's fixed 3 s: a far station cannot answer, or report a failure, before it has finished receiving, and a Raspberry Pi 4 takes up to about 1.55 times a dense burst's airtime to do that (3.7 s for a 2.4 s burst), so a fixed 3 s would count a silence against a station still decoding. It is a starting value, to be replaced when stations can tell each other how fast they decode (packet-net/pdn-soundmodem#883). "Read" is the station's clock when the header read is taken in, which is a detector frame boundary, so inline the answer does not depend on the block size; a reply that began inside the wait may be read after it and counts too late.
- **The simulated channel** (test project). Stations in one process on one sample clock, all inline. It cuts every step at the next thing that can happen to any station (the end of the block, a timer due, the end of the detector's frame, its own scheduled starts, ends, carrier indications and deaf spells), so a grant or withdrawal is raised at the last sample of a `Push`, and the sim's `TransmitStarted` and `TransmitEnded` are called at exactly the reported clock, whatever the block size. Per pair: gain, FM-shaped noise (flat to 300 Hz, rising 20 dB a decade above, from a stream seeded per pair), delay in whole samples, clock offset in ppm (the resampler). Per station, all off by default: receive and transmit low-pass (4th-order Butterworth), receive gain, key-up delay (the first samples of what is played are not radiated), deafness after its own transmission, the audio layer's delay before it keys a request, and a carrier indication to `SetExternalBusy` after a delay. A station does not hear while it transmits; simultaneous signals add. The test seams `Station.NextTimerDue` and `Station.SamplesToNextFrame` (internal) exist for it. A withdrawal raised before the audio layer would have keyed takes the request back; at or after that sample it has started anyway.
- **Block-size independence.** Events raised in the call that causes them (requests, withdrawals, `ChannelBusy`, `Transmitted`) carry the same clock whatever the block size; events from applying a received burst's result (`Received`, `PathReport`, `LinkState`) are raised at the end of the call that finds the result ready, so their clock moves with the block, and their text and order do not. The determinism test compares both. (Step 6 changed this for an inline station: it applies results at a frame's end, so those clocks no longer move with the block; see "Step 6 as built".)

### Findings

- **A reply in the first slot is not heard** by the station that just transmitted (fixed in step 6, see "Step 6 as built"). Step 4 ignores candidates for 0.25 s after a station's own transmission; an expedited reply goes 50 ms after the channel clears and its first training sample is the 88 ms lead-in later, 138 ms after the burst ended. With no radio turn-round to excuse it (a simulated channel with every knob off) the acknowledgement is lost. Pinned by `Records_Present_Behaviour_A_Reply_In_The_First_Slot_Is_Not_Heard_By_The_Station_That_Just_Transmitted`; the figure for the design to settle is 0.25 s against 0.138 s (#883 item 8).
- **Traffic one way stays at the first-contact format** (until step 6: now it climbs). The receiver measures the path and would recommend a fast format, but the recommendation reaches the sender only in a burst from the receiver; with nothing to send, that is the lone report of step 6. Every burst asks for a report and is met with silence.
- **Unlike radios** (`StationUnlikeRadioTests`, gated, 40 datagrams each way, a wide station and one whose receive audio is cut at 3 kHz): at a good path the wide station sends the narrow one formats reaching above 3 kHz for 37 of 40 bursts and 7 are lost, spread through the run (not early only), against none lost the other way and none with no cut. The narrow station's estimate of the path falls from 39 dB at 2 kHz to 17 dB at 6 kHz, so it does learn, but its recommendation stays on the 6.5 and 8 kHz bands (formats 28, 61, 62). At 20 dB down, 5 of 40 (and 1 the other way); at 30 dB down the estimate shows nothing above 3 kHz, the wide station settles on the 3 kHz band (format 158) and nothing is lost. The adaptation rules are unchanged; the numbers are pinned as present behaviour.

## Step 6 as built

Reports (`Station.Reports.cs`): what a verdict owes, the lone report, what follows it, and the hint to the host. Where the design or the protocol left room, or the build differs:

- **The fault found in step 5 (a reply in the first slot was not heard) is fixed.** The mute after the station's own transmission (`Station.MuteAfterOwnTransmission`) is now **100 ms (4800 samples), the same figure as the own tail the channel stays busy (`ChannelAccess.OwnTail`)**, where step 4 had the design's 0.25 s. A reply in the first slot has its first training sample 2400 (50 ms of clear channel) plus 4224 (the lead-in) samples, 138 ms, after the end of the burst it answers, so the mute must end before that: it ends 38 ms before, a margin. The prototype's stations heard each other's first-slot reports on the bench; how long a real radio takes to recover is packet-net/pdn-soundmodem#891's question and not decided here. The pinned test became `A_Reply_In_The_First_Slot_Is_Heard_By_The_Station_That_Just_Transmitted`.
- **What a verdict owes.** For each neighbour the station holds at most one report it owes and has not built, the newest replacing the one before. Three kinds. **Failed**: a payload addressed to this station failed; it goes alone, as soon as the channel has been clear for 50 ms, however late the verdict is, and data does not cancel it. **Drop**: a burst addressed to this station decoded and the rules returned a "drop"; it goes alone in the first slot, and is not sent if data to that station that carries a recommendation goes first. **Asked**: the burst decoded and its prelude said "report wanted" (and the rules hold a recommendation; peeked, not taken); the recommendation rides on whatever the host sends back to that station, and goes alone once **1.5 s** (`Station.AskedReportWaitSamples`, the prototype's figure, fixed) have passed from the end of the burst, which is the protocol's "once the slot has passed" given a number. Nothing is owed for a burst addressed to everyone or to another station (the rules return nothing for those and the station asks for nothing). The rules return no report when they have no recommendation to give, and then nothing is sent.
- **Replacing.** A newer failed or drop verdict replaces an older unsent one, whatever its kind. A requested report is owed only if nothing else is. A burst that decodes and owes nothing leaves an unsent failed report in place as a report of the recommendation alone (step 8 changed this: the failed bit says "your last burst", which a newer burst that decoded makes untrue): the sender is still owed the recommendation. (An edge that remains: if the sender has sent another burst in the meantime and the report was built before that burst's verdict was applied, the hint, below, is for that burst and not the one that failed.)
- **`ReportFor` is called at the moment the recommendation goes on the air**, never when the report is owed: in the prelude of a data burst when it is built (at the grant), and for a requested lone report when it is built (at the grant). A failed or drop report carries what the rules returned with the verdict, which they have already recorded as told. A burst once built is kept, as in step 5: a withdrawn report is handed over again unchanged.
- **Data going first.** When the station builds a data burst for a neighbour and the prelude carries a recommendation, a drop or requested report owed to that neighbour is dropped (counted in the internal `ReportsCovered`): the recommendation, the current one, has gone. This is a little wider than the protocol's "the same recommendation" (a recommendation that has risen since would have been sent the older, lower figure after the newer). When the report request is granted and the host has an expedited datagram queued for the neighbour, which goes in the same slot, the report is dropped and the datagram goes first with the recommendation in its prelude; a failed report is never dropped. Ordinary data goes 0.8 s or more after the channel was last busy, so it rides on a requested report within its 1.5 s and never beats a drop report; a datagram built before the verdict was applied carries the older recommendation (the verdict is applied up to a frame after the burst ends), and the drop report follows it.
- **What the report is.** `BurstRenderer.RenderReport` of a report header (the recommended format and the failed bit). **It opens the way this station's own data to that neighbour would and at the same drive**: the format `LinkAdaptation.Choose(dest, now)` gives, its opening and its drive. `Choose` is not free of effects in the rules (it creates the neighbour's record, and drops one unheard for ten minutes), neither of which matters here (the neighbour has just been heard). Only its format is used: not its "report wanted" and nothing of it is recorded. `ModemSent` is raised when it goes on air, with the format that opening was chosen from (not the recommendation).
- **One request for all reports.** The channel-access rules hold one request, class Report, for every report that is ready (a requested one is ready 1.5 s after its burst); when granted it takes the oldest ready, first in first out. The timer for the next requested report is on the sample clock, so it is exact.
- **After a lone report.** The station that heard it leaves ordinary traffic for a second (step 4). The station that sent it goes straight on with its own data to that neighbour, if it has any queued and is not waiting for an answer to a burst of its own (an answer wait in progress with nothing heard from that station since the burst ended: `AwaitingAnswerFrom`): `ChannelAccess.GoStraightOn` replaces the ordinary wait (0.8 s and a drawn number of slots) by the first slot's 50 ms, for the clear period that follows the report only. It goes 150 ms after the report's last sample (the 100 ms tail and the 50 ms), measured.
- **A report heard** is `HeardReport`, `PathReport`, the answer wait satisfied (any header read from that station counts, as in step 5), and `LinkState` raised (its knowledge, "report", follows the reports). If it says failed: **`BurstNotDecoded`**, raised after `PathReport`, with the tags of the datagrams in the latest burst this station sent to that neighbour **whose last sample is not after the report's first training sample**, once for that burst. "Cannot": the burst ended (by the audio layer's `TransmitEnded`) after the report began, so the other station had not finished receiving it. Per neighbour the station keeps the tags and end of the last burst only, so a report about an older burst, with a newer one sent in between, raises nothing. A report cannot begin inside the station's own transmission or the 100 ms after it (it is not heard), so the case arises only when a burst is keyed over the opening of a report not yet found.
- **Results are applied at a frame's end by an inline station.** The lone report's start must not depend on how the audio was cut into blocks, and results were applied at the end of whichever `Push` found them ready. An inline station now applies its results (`Received`, `PathReport`, `LinkState`, the calls on the rules, and so what they start) at the end of the correlation frame, the same sample whatever the block size: up to a frame (0.42 s) after a burst's last sample, where before it was after the next block. A station with workers applies them at the end of the call that finds them ready, as before (their time depends on the workers). The determinism test compares the log of every event at both stations, clocks included, at blocks of 1000, 4800 and 100000.
- **A seam for the test: `Station.ReceivesHeld`** (internal) holds every burst's receive job while set, header reads still going, so that a verdict can land as late as a test says without any real time (the sample clock and the seam only). Step 3's `JobStarting` hook parks a job on a worker thread, which an inline simulated channel cannot use.
- **Counters for the tests** (internal): `ReportsOwedCount` (failed, drop, requested), `ReportsCovered`, `ReportsBuilt`.

## Step 7 as built

Identification (`Station.Identification.cs`, `PayloadCodec`, the renderer and the receive path). Where the design left room, or the build differs:

- **The rule changed on 27 November 2026** (Tom: "we don't need a closing identification"; the Morse ident in pdn-soundmodem is being changed the same way). The first form of step 7 also had a timer that made an idle station key once to cover what it had sent after its last identification; that is gone, with the "transmitted since" flag. The rest of this section is the rule as it now stands.
- **The rule, in plain words** (the host program's existing Morse ident follows the same one). An identification is due when a transmission of the station's (data or a lone report, not an identification burst) ends and either the station has never identified or ten minutes of the sample clock have passed since its last identification burst started. Nothing else makes one due: there is no identification timer. "The last identification" is counted from when its burst started on the air (`TransmitStarted`), not from when it fell due, so a long wait for the channel does not shorten the next interval. As built:
  - **A station that never transmits never identifies.**
  - **A station that has been quiet and then transmits** (ten minutes or more since its last identification, or none ever) is due when that transmission ends: it identifies right after it, by the ordinary access rules, ahead of host traffic. A station that transmits once therefore identifies once, immediately, and then owes nothing.
  - **A station that keeps transmitting** identifies after the first transmission that ends ten minutes or more after the start of its last identification burst. Transmissions that end sooner are covered by that identification and owe nothing later.
  - **An idle station never keys to identify.** A station that identified, transmitted again within the ten minutes and went quiet stays quiet, however long the clock runs.
  - The identification burst is not a transmission for this rule: it does not make another one owed. A lone report is a transmission.
- **The state.** `_lastIdentStart` (the sample the last identification burst started at) and the flag "owed". No timer and no "transmitted since" flag. When a non-identification transmission ends (inside the call to `TransmitEnded`), the station compares that sample with `_lastIdentStart` plus the interval.
- **When it falls due.** `IdentificationDue` (for the audio layer's Morse ident, the only thing the station exposes for it) is raised in that same call to `TransmitEnded`, at the moment the rule makes it due. The station is then not keyed. At that moment the burst is queued in the lone-report class, so it goes ahead of the host's datagrams, in the first slot once the channel has been clear for 50 ms (after the end of a transmission: the 100 ms tail and the 50 ms). The order a host sees for one identification: `IdentificationDue`, `TransmitRequest`, then, in the audio layer's call to `TransmitStarted`, `ModemSent` (kind `Identification`, the format). Each is raised once per identification.
- **A busy channel.** The identification stays owed for as long as the channel is busy (another station's burst, the host's carrier indication, the station's own burst in progress); nothing expires it and `IdentificationDue` is not raised again. The burst is built when granted and kept if withdrawn, like a data burst. Anything sent while it is owed is covered by it and starts no new count.
- **The payload** (in the protocol document, "The identification payload"): one length byte, the callsign, the capability word (the station's own, `CapabilityWord`, 0), then tagged items to the end, skipped if unknown, ended by a tag of 0 (the padding) or an item that runs off the end. The callsign is 1 to 16 characters of A to Z, 0 to 9, "/" and "-": 16 is `VK2/M0LTE/QRP-15`, and takes 20 payload symbols (1.28 s) at the first-contact format; up to nine characters took 12 symbols (0.92 s) before the station id (item 1, 4 bytes plus its 2-byte header) was added in step two of the station numbers: 16 symbols (1.10 s) now, and 24 (1.46 s) for the longest callsign. Upper case only, so that every station that names a station by its callsign writes it one way. `SetIdentity` refuses a callsign outside these rules (it goes on the air as given). `PayloadCodec.PackIdentification` throws on an invalid callsign, a capability word outside 16 bits or a bad item; `TryUnpackIdentification` never throws and never allocates, and says no for anything that is not a callsign and a word.
- **The renderer.** `BurstRenderer.RenderData` takes an identification header (burst type 2) as well as a data header: the same path, the payload scrambled and checked exactly as a data burst to everyone is, since the seed is the sender, the destination (0) and the burst number, all in the header, and the receiver needs nothing else. No new member; an identification header whose destination is not 0 is refused, as is a report. `BurstHeader.Identification(sender, format, payloadSymbols, burstNumber)` makes the header. The receivers (`PayloadReceiver`, `TrackedReceiver`) read an identification header like a data header (their one test of the burst type accepts both).
- **Burst number.** An identification burst takes the station's next burst number, from the same count as data bursts, so that each identification is a different waveform (the scrambler's seed is the reason the number exists). The receiver does nothing with it.
- **Receiving one.** The station's header read takes type 2 as a burst with a payload, to everyone: the opening must be its format's, as for data, and a destination other than 0 is noted as invalid and not received. The burst is received like any other, then applied in the order the bursts started: `HeardData` with destination everyone (so the burst is measured exactly as a burst to everyone is, and nothing is owed: the rules return no report for it) and the capability word if the payload read, which the rules keep against that station. A payload that decoded but is not a callsign and a word is measured and raises nothing.
- **The name.** (Superseded on 27 November 2026: a heard callsign names nothing and none of this is done; see "Link addresses as built".) The callsign becomes the number's name unless the host named the number (`Bind` always wins, and its names are never replaced); a callsign the host bound to another number is the host's and is not taken from it; one learned at another number moves to the latest, as `Bind` moves a name; a number's learned name is replaced by a later callsign from it; a callsign equal to the station's own is nobody else's name. `Bind` replaces a learned name, as it always replaced one. A name that changes is announced once in a `LinkState` under the new name, as after a `Bind`; events raised earlier keep the placeholder.
- **The event.** A new small event, `Identified` (`IdentifiedEvent`: callsign, capability word; it carried the station number and the name too until 27 November 2026), not `Received` with a marker: `Received` is for datagrams, a link layer must not be handed an identification as one, and a marker would make every host test for it. It is raised for each identification that decoded and read, in the order the bursts started, and says what the station sent.
- **Station constructor.** Superseded on 27 November 2026: the constructor takes no callsign (see "No identity" below).
- **The interval and the test seam.** The interval is a fixed value, `Station.IdentificationIntervalSamples` (internal): 600 s of the sample clock, the interval of the host program's existing Morse ident, which the project owner chose. The tests step the clock with a shorter interval through an internal property, `Station.IdentificationInterval`, set before the station first transmits. That is sound because the interval is read in one place (when a transmission ends) and goes only into one comparison, so nothing else changes with it, and the real value is pinned by a test of the constant (28 800 000 samples) and run end to end, the whole ten minutes stepped, behind `SM_MASK_GATE=1` (ten minutes of audio through the detector costs several seconds of processor time and the always-on tier does not pay for it).

### No identity (27 November 2026)

The callsign is no longer fixed at construction. As built:

- `Station.SetIdentity(callsign)` returns `IdentityResult`: `Accepted` (the first valid callsign, or the same one again, which changes nothing), `Invalid` (not 1 to 16 characters of A to Z, 0 to 9, "/" and "-": the same `PayloadCodec.IsValidCallsign` the payload uses) or `Different` (another callsign once one is set; nothing changes). There is no way to clear it; to change callsign a host makes a new station. `Callsign` is null until set.
- Without an identity the station is a listener only. It finds bursts, decodes, measures, runs the adaptation rules and raises `Received`, `PathReport`, `LinkState`, `Identified`, `ChannelBusy` and the rest as before. It never asks to transmit.
- `Send` returns `SendResult.NoIdentity`, checked before every other refusal (after the argument check, which throws), and queues nothing. A verdict owes no report: no failed, drop or requested report is recorded, so nothing stale goes out when an identity is set later. The rules are still called, and what they return is dropped. No identification can fall due, since the station has not transmitted.
- Structurally, `RequestTransmit`, the one place a request is put to the audio layer, throws `InvalidOperationException` for a station with no identity. The checks above keep every path from getting there.
- The identification rule, its clock and the Morse ident (`IdentificationDue`) are unchanged. What a station learns from other stations' identification bursts is unchanged; a station with no identity has no callsign to compare them with, so every callsign heard is somebody else's.

Not decided here: how stations come by their numbers (#835): a number learned from an identification is only as good as the number's uniqueness on the channel, which nothing yet guarantees.

## Step 8 as built

The simulated channel learns what an FM receiver does when signals overlap, three stations share it, and the scenarios of K step 8 are run, as seeded runs on the sample clock (`Hostile*Tests` in the test project; the figures below are printed by them, and `SM_HOSTILE_OUT=<file>` also writes them to a file). **The library changed in one place**, to fix a bug the soak found (see Bugs): the public API and its approved snapshot are as they were. Everything else the step added is in the test project, plus this section. **All the figures are about the station's logic under a stated, assumed model of the channel. They are not predictions for a real channel**: nothing about the capture effect has been measured on a radio (packet-net/pdn-soundmodem#835 is where it will be).

### The channel model (assumed, `CaptureModel` in `SimChannel.cs`)

Per receiver and per instant, from the received levels. Each signal's level is its root mean square over the last 20 ms (960 samples), after the path's gain; a signal below 1e-5 (a burst's lead-in) is silence and takes no part. The strongest is compared with the power sum of the others:

- **Stronger by at least the capture margin (default 3 dB):** the receiver's audio is the strongest signal's, and the others become white Gaussian noise at their own level less `10 + 1 x (difference - margin)` dB (the suppression at the margin is 10 dB, and each further dB of difference takes another dB off). The noise is a seeded stream per receiver.
- **Within the margin:** the audio is unusable, white noise at the level of all the signals together.

The three parameters are named (`MarginDb` 3, `SuppressionBaseDb` 10, `SuppressionSlope` 1), the old adder is still there (`SimChannel.Capture` null, the default, so every earlier test is unchanged), and the gated tier runs the scenarios at margins of 1, 3 and 6 dB. A station that keys over another (`SimChannel.Interfere`) and an observer for a station's start (`TransmissionStarted`) are the other additions, and `SimNode.RecordInput` keeps what a receiver was given.

### Bugs

**One, found by the soak, fixed: a hint that named a datagram that had arrived.** Seed 5, a mean gap of 2 s, detection alone: A sent C two short bursts 0.9 s apart, C could not decode the first and decoded the second, and the "failed" report C sent afterwards made A's `BurstNotDecoded` name the second burst's datagram (the one that arrived), while the one that was lost was named by nobody. The failed bit means "your last burst reached me and I could not decode it", and two faults in the plumbing made it false: (1) a failed report owed for an older burst stayed failed after a newer burst from the same station decoded; it is now sent as the recommendation alone, as a drop report is (the host's hint is not raised for it); (2) the report was built the moment its verdict was applied, because a clear channel grants the request before `Enqueue` returns, so a newer burst's verdict applied in the same batch came too late to take it back; the request with the channel-access rules now waits until the batch of results has been applied (`Station.Reports.cs`, `Station.Receive.cs`). The test is `StationFailedReportTests` (it names the burst that arrived before the fix and does not after; no other test changed). What is left of the edge is the one the report's wire form cannot settle: a report built before the newer burst's verdict is applied (the verdict comes up to 0.42 s after a burst's end, a report can go 0.15 s after it) still says failed about an older burst, and the hint then names the newer one. It did not occur in the nine soak runs after the fix (every hint named a datagram that was sent to that station and did not arrive).

What else was looked for, by checks in the soak that fail on a fault, and not found: a datagram lost with no `Dropped` or `Transmitted`, delivered twice, delivered but not announced as transmitted, a hint that names a datagram that was not sent to that station, a path report that nothing explains, ordinary datagrams reordered, a log clock going backwards, a station that stops transmitting, queues, candidates, timers or bursts in flight that grow, an exception.

### Poor behaviour, recorded and not tuned

Each is pinned by a test whose name says it records present behaviour (or by the scenario's own assertions), because the rules and the access rules' fixed values are not this step's to change.

1. **Two stations given a datagram within the detection time on an idle channel both key.** An ordinary datagram waits for the channel to have been clear for 0.8 s plus the drawn slots, and the draw is made when the channel was last busy, so on a channel that has been idle for longer the wait is already over and the station goes at once. Two stations given a datagram at the same sample both go at the same sample (collided every time, with or without a carrier indication). With detection alone 0.2 s apart still collides, 0.5 s apart does not (the second station notices the first within 0.5 s here, the 0.9 s of step 4 being the worst case); with a carrier indication 0.2 s apart does not. The soak's bursts collided at 26 and 7 percent (a mean gap of 9 s between a station's datagrams, detection alone and with an indication), 46 and 28 percent (4 s) and 58 and 48 percent (3 s), each one seed. A link that has collisions slows (the silence steps and the first-contact format make bursts longer) and so collides more: at gaps of 4 s and less the formats station A sends at stay mostly in the 100s and 180s where the quiet runs reach the 30s (28 to 31).
2. **A collision where the payload begins is read as the path failing, and at the first-contact format a collision part-way is never read as sudden.** The sudden test needs a clean early part: its median miss under 1.5 times the opening's plus 0.5. At the first-contact format the payload's clean symbols miss by 0.5 to 10 (median 4.2 in the first eight symbols of a burst that decoded, against the opening's 1.8, so the limit is 3.2), so the early part is never clean and a failure 0.2 s into the payload is a plain failure: B raised its margin for A from 3.00 to 3.50 and set the cap (at 192 the cap changes nothing, at any other format the recommendation would have fallen). A collision from the start of the payload, or one the test cannot see, costs half a dB of margin each time (to 12). The same noisy miss at the first-contact format is the likely cause of packet-net/pdn-soundmodem#898 (clean bursts judged sudden). Where the format is dense (format 124, scenario 3) the test works at every position.

   **27 November 2026, packet-net/pdn-soundmodem#898 fixed.** The first-contact miss scatters because of the sender's peak limit, not the noise, and the judgement now compares the later part with the worst of the earlier part and with the opening ("The sudden judgement, as fixed", below). A collision two symbols or more into a first-contact payload is judged sudden: C starting 0.5 s after A, at equal strength or 20 dB stronger, leaves B's margin for A at 3.00 and sets no cap (`A_Collision_Two_Symbols_Into_A_First_Contact_Payload_Is_Judged_Sudden...`). What remains: a collision where the payload begins (C 0.43 s after A, its signal under one symbol into the payload) is still read as the path failing, as above, since there is no clean part to compare with; and a sudden failure at first contact owes no report (B has no estimate, so no recommendation to repeat), so the sender is told nothing and counts a silence.
3. **A stronger burst that begins inside a burst the receiver already knows is ignored unread** (step 3's rule that a candidate more than a symbol into a known burst is not read). Under capture the receiver is hearing the stronger one: A's payload is lost and C's burst is thrown away, so neither arrives (scenario 2, C 20 dB stronger and 0.5 s later).
4. **Hearing the common neighbour talk to someone else counts as an answer.** A hidden station C asked for a report and was lost under A's burst; B sent A a lone report, C heard it, and any header read from B satisfies C's answer wait, so C counted no silence and kept its format. The protocol counts a silence unless a report for this station came. Where B said nothing at all, C counted one.
5. **A requested report, a timer, can key over a burst that has just started.** In a flood of 300-byte datagrams one way over a clean path, B's 1.5 s requested report went 0.14 s after A's next burst began (too soon for B to have noticed it); A's burst of three datagrams was lost without a hint, and 3 of the 5 datagrams accepted never arrived. A burst sent over a recent report ("goes straight on") and a report timer are two stations each doing what the rules say.
6. **A cut turns the two stations into hidden stations to each other**, which collides their bursts at the third. Seen in some runs, not in the run recorded here (seed 3: collisions at C 2, 0 and 2 before, during and after; seeds 1, 2, 4, 7 and 8 collided 6 to 12 bursts at C in the cut, and the control run without the cut had 2, 0 and 0), and C's recommendation for A moved with or without it (157 to 189 with the cut, 157 to 31 without): it follows which formats A's bursts to C used, not the cut.

7. **An address its host has never sent from cannot be located** (added with link addresses, 27 November 2026). After three silences the station forgets where an address lives and sends to everyone at the first-contact format, with no report asked for. The far station answers such a burst with an announcement when its own host has sent from the address named in For, and every station that hears it learns the address, so one-way traffic is addressed after one burst and climbs again (`A_Flood_For_An_Address_Whose_Host_Has_Sent_From_It_Is_Answered_With_An_Announcement...`, and the next flood after a forgetting relearns it). What remains: a host that has never sent from an address (nothing of its own has gone out from it yet, so the station does not answer for it) cannot be located, so traffic to it stays at first contact until it does, and so does traffic to an address no station owns. In the path-cut scenario C's host therefore sends A one datagram at the start: without it, on seed 2 a burst for C lost to a collision early on left A at first contact to C for the whole run.

### What did not fit

- The protocol's table has "A header, a payload that fails, and quality good at the start and falling suddenly" and "nothing"; real overlaps give a third case at the first-contact format (poor behaviour 2), and under capture "nothing" is also what a stronger burst that came inside a known one gives the receiver of the stronger burst (3). **27 November 2026:** the third case came from the sudden test, which is fixed (packet-net/pdn-soundmodem#898); a collision where the payload begins is still read as the path failing, which no test of this kind can tell apart, and a sudden failure at first contact sends no report.
- The design's answer wait (any header from the station, step 5) and the protocol's silence (a report, or a burst of theirs) differ (4).
- The access rules were designed for stations that notice each other's carriers within a time, and the idle channel has no wait at all (1): the design's F gives "clear for T101 plus a random T102, drawn afresh each time the channel was last busy", which says nothing for the case of a channel that has not been busy.
- A weaker interferer never spoilt a burst at format 124 at any margin tried (10 dB weaker, residual noise 24 to 27 dB under the burst); the case that does is nearer, and is in the gated sweep at 4 dB weaker. **27 November 2026:** that case (residual noise 15 dB under the burst from half way) was judged sudden at margins 3 and 6 and not at 1; with the sudden judgement fixed it is judged sudden at 6 only, and at 1 and 3 B reads it as the path failing (recommendation 123). Its miss rises about threefold, the most a dense constellation's miss shows, which is on the edge of any threefold test ("The sudden judgement, as fixed", what it costs).

## Link addresses as built

Step one of docs/station-numbers.md, built on 27 November 2026: the host's side of the station is by link address, and the station learns where each address lives. Step two (random station numbers, the station id, the Clash item, a number changing hands) was built later the same day: see "Station numbers as built". Where this section and the sections on steps 3 to 8 differ, this one is later.

What was built:

- **`LinkAddress`** replaces `StationName`: 1 to 32 opaque bytes, copied in, equal when the bytes are equal, an ASCII convenience (`FromAscii`), and `Everyone` as a value of its own. Its `Key` is the payload's own CRC-32 over its bytes, four bytes on the air; the protocol document has a worked example, and a test pins it.
- **On the air.** Every data burst that carries datagrams has a From item (runs of 6 bytes: the key of the address the next datagrams were sent from, their protocol byte and how many; a new run when either changes or a count would pass 255): in the prelude's extension block for a burst to one station, and in a leading block, which a burst to everyone now has, with a For item when the sender has not located the address. An identification burst is untouched. The layout, with worked examples, is in the protocol document, "Link addresses".
- **Learning** (`AddressTable`, internal): key to station number and when heard, written for every key in every data burst decoded, whoever it is for, so a burst for another station is unpacked far enough to read its block. The latest burst heard wins. An item with a tag this version does not know is skipped, and a From or For of the wrong length is skipped; a station never learns from an identification burst. Once an address is in the table, learning it again allocates nothing.
- **Forgetting.** When the station an address lives at is forgotten (`CheckGone`, ten minutes unheard), every address at it goes with it; the table is also checked against the station's last-heard time whenever an address is looked up, so no address is used after the ten minutes whether or not `CheckGone` has run yet. When a third burst running to a station has met silence (`OnAnswerTimer`, `Silences` of 3 or more), every address at it is forgotten. A further silence, which the first-contact format still counts until a report is heard, forgets again. The host is told by `LinkState` with `Located` false and `Gone` true.
- **Sending.** `Send(datagram, to, from, tag, reportWanted, priority)`; `Bind` and `SendResult.NotBound` are gone (the values after it moved up one). There is one queue for each address `to`. When a burst is built at the grant, the station looks up where the address lives: located, the burst goes to that station exactly as before, with the From item; not located, it goes to everyone at the first-contact format with For set and From, with no prelude, so "report wanted" has no effect and no answer is waited for. A burst is built for one address, so a burst to everyone carries datagrams for one For, or for none, and two addresses at one station go in separate bursts. The lookup is made when the burst is built, so datagrams waiting for an address follow it when it becomes located or is forgotten: none is lost or duplicated by the change, and a burst once built is kept as the withdrawal rule says.
- **Capacity.** Everything that sizes a burst counts the block: the room a datagram has (`TooLarge`, `LinkState.DatagramRoom`), the packing and the 2.5 s ceiling (6 bytes for each run in the burst, at most 40 runs), and the 30 s queue bound (the queue's bytes, and the block the runs of everything queued need). The figures at the first-contact format, from a longest burst of 348 bytes: 333 bytes to a located address (was 342: the prelude's 4, the block's 9 with one run, and the length's 2), 331 to an address not located (the block is 15 with the For; no prelude), 337 to everyone (was 346).
- **Receiving.** A burst addressed to this station is delivered; a burst to everyone is delivered whether or not it has a For, and the modem does not filter. `Received` carries, for each datagram, `FromKey` and `Protocol` (from its run), and `Addressing` (to this station, to everyone, or for an address not yet located) and `ForKey`. A burst whose runs are not whole (no From, a length that is not whole runs, counts that do not add up to the datagrams) is malformed: nothing is learned from it and nothing delivered from it (it is still measured as any burst is; the simplest safe behaviour). Only the first From item counts.
- **Protocol byte.** `Send` takes a protocol byte, carried in the run. The modem does nothing else with it; `LinkProtocol` names the values the protocol document lists (0x00 whole AX.25 frame, 0xCC IP, 0xCD ARP, 0xCF NET/ROM, 0xF0 no layer 3).
- **A key in place of an address.** `LinkAddress.FromKey(key)` makes an address from a key alone (no bytes; equal only to another made from that key). `Send` and `Cancel` take it in place of the full address: the station keeps one queue for each key, so it reaches the same queue and the same place. Events carry the address that first made the queue, whichever form that was.
- **Answering for its own addresses: the announcement** (`Station.Reports.cs`, `Station.Receive.cs`). The station keeps the keys its host has sent from. A decoded burst to everyone whose For is one of them owes the sender an announcement, one for each (station, key), a newer flood replacing it: a data burst to that station with the recommendation in its prelude (if there is one), "report wanted" not set, a From item of one run (the key, protocol 0, count 0) and no datagrams. It is owed like a requested report: a data burst built for that station within `AskedReportWaitSamples` of the flood's end whose From runs include the key covers it; otherwise it goes alone when the wait runs out, in the report class, at the format `Choose` gives for that station. It raises `ModemSent` (kind `Announcement`) and is a transmission like any other. Everyone who decodes it learns the address by the ordinary learning; nothing is delivered. A count of 0 in a burst with datagrams is skipped when attributing them. **A first form was withdrawn**: the sender bound the address of the one flood outstanding to the station a lone report came from. The path-cut scenario on a cut of 8 rounds showed it on seeds 5, 6 and 7: C's lone report about A's datagrams to C arrived while a flood for B's address was outstanding, and A then sent B's datagrams addressed to station 3 (C). A report cannot say what it answers, so nothing is bound from a report any more; `A_Lone_Report_From_A_Third_Station_While_A_Flood_Is_Outstanding_Binds_Nothing` pins it.
- **Events by address.** `Transmitted`, `Dropped` (by tag), `LinkState`, `Congestion`, `PathReport`, `BurstNotDecoded` and `Snapshot` speak of the address the host sent to. An event about a station is raised once for each address the host has sent to that currently lives there (`PathReport`, `LinkState`); a station nobody sent to raises none. `BurstNotDecoded` is raised once, for the address the burst was sent to. `ModemSent` no longer names a station.
- **`LinkState` in both directions.** `RateBitsPerSecond` is the rate this station sends to the address at; `RecommendedRateBitsPerSecond` is the rate of the format this station has recommended to the station the address lives at (what it has told them the path from them supports: the rules' `Told`), null when nothing has been recommended yet and for an address not located. It is raised again when it changes, which happens when a burst or a lone report carries a recommendation.
- **`LinkState` for an address not located.** `Located` is false; the format and rate are null, the knowledge is first contact, the age 0 and the room is that of a burst to everyone for one address (333); `Gone` is true when the address was located and has been forgotten. There is one for each address the host has sent to, raised when it first sends and when what is known changes. `Snapshot` lists them in the order the addresses were first sent to, and leaves out everyone.
- **Test seams.** The rigs pre-locate every address in the same way a conversation would (`Station.Locate`, internal), so the scenarios keep their flows. The station number and the sender of a received datagram are read by the simulated channel through internal members (`HearingSender`, `ModemSentTo`), since no host sees them.

The invariants each test asserts hold: no datagram twice, none lost without a hint or a count, no station stuck, no queue left. The receiver's test for "the quality collapsed part-way" is noisy at the first-contact format (packet-net/pdn-soundmodem#898): which clean first bursts it judges sudden, and so takes no measurement from, depends on the burst's bits, so several scenarios were re-run with other datagram contents or seeds, as listed. In the Hostile scenarios a datagram is 25 bytes where it was 30 (`Ledger.DefaultLength`), which keeps those bursts close to their old length. **27 November 2026:** the sudden judgement is fixed and the contents and lengths changed for it are back as they were (30 bytes, datagrams 100 up); "The sudden judgement, as fixed" lists them.

Choices the design left open, taken as the simplest that fits it:

1. At most 40 runs in one burst's From (an item holds up to 42; this leaves room for For in the same block).
2. A burst is for one address only, as above.
3. The queue bound counts the block with the runs of everything queued (expedited first), not the prelude.
4. The ten-minute rule is the station's, not each address's: an address is forgotten with the station, whichever burst of the station was heard last. The table also keeps when each address was heard, and nothing reads it yet.
5. A datagram accepted for a located address can be dropped with `NoLongerFits` if the address is forgotten before it goes, since a burst to everyone for an address has 2 bytes less room than one to a located address at the first-contact format.
6. A station answers for an address only after its host has sent from it once: the keys it answers for are the keys of `Send`'s `from`, and nothing is learned about them from the air. A host that has never sent from an address cannot be located (poor behaviour 7). The announcement goes alone after the requested-report wait (1.5 s), not at once, so that a reply from the host can carry it.
7. `Send` throws for an empty `to` and for an empty or everyone `from`.

## Station numbers as built

Step two of docs/station-numbers.md, built on 27 November 2026 (`Station.Numbers.cs`, with changes in `Station.Receive.cs`, `Station.Identification.cs`, `Station.Reports.cs`, `Station.Send.cs`, `ChannelAccess.cs` and `LinkAdaptation.cs`). The constructor is `Station(Random random, bool runJobsInline = false)`; no member of the host interface mentions a number or an id (a test reads the public surface). Where this section and an earlier one differ, this one is later.

What was built:

- **A number and an id at the first accepted `SetIdentity`.** `PickNumber` takes a number at random from those not heard as the sender of a burst in the last ten minutes of the sample clock (a number heard exactly ten minutes ago still counts as heard, as a neighbour is not forgotten until it is more than ten minutes), and `NewId` 32 random bits, both from the station's own `Random`. If all 63 were heard, the one heard longest ago (the lowest on a tie). The number the station is leaving never counts as free. "Heard" is a table of the sample clock at each header read (`_senderHeardAt`), written for every header read whatever its kind or destination, before anything else is done with it. Without an identity the station has no number (0), no id, and `mine` is false for every burst; it still measures, learns and forgets.
- **The id on the air.** An identification burst has item 1, four bytes most significant first, straight after the capability word. The station reads it back for the sender's number into `_idValue`, with when the burst ended. The worked examples are in the protocol document, and tests pin the bytes.
- **Hearing its own number.** In the header read (`HandleHeader`, audio thread), a header whose sender is this station's number moves the station unless it is transmitting or inside the mute tail of its own transmission (`InOwnWindow`). The burst is then taken in as the other station's. Nothing is announced.
- **Moving.** `MoveToNewNumber` takes a number by the rule above, a new id, and tells the adaptation rules its new number. What the station knows of others is untouched. Whatever it had built and not put on the air carries the old number in its header and the seed of its scrambler, so it is **discarded and built again** (the choice the brief offered against patching, and the simpler): `DiscardBuiltBursts`. A data burst's datagrams go back to the front of their queues, in order, expedited first, so none is lost or reordered; a lone report goes back to what is owed (a requested one as a "drop" report carrying the recommendation it had); an announcement goes back to the announcements owed; an identification and a Clash are simply built again. The request is taken back from the audio layer (`ChannelAccess.TakeBack`, the same as the withdrawal for a busy channel) and the rules grant it again, which they do at once if the channel is clear. A burst already on the air is the audio layer's and is left. Two small things are not restored: an announcement that a data burst had covered stays covered (the new burst carries its From key just the same), and `ReportFor` has marked the neighbour as told, which a rebuilt prelude does not need.
- **One rule for a changed id** (`NoteStationId`, in `ApplyIdentification`, before the burst is measured so that a new station is measured afresh). A different id on a number with a known id: `ForgetNumber` forgets the neighbour record, the addresses learned at it (the host is told `Gone` through `LinkState`), what was owed to it (reports, announcements, the answer waits, the record of what was sent to it) and what was queued for it. The datagrams queued for addresses located there, and those in a burst built for it, are dropped with `DropReason.LinkForgotten`, which was documented as not raised and now is for this case alone. A Clash is owed if the earlier id was heard within ten minutes (`OweClash`). A station with no identity forgets but cannot owe.
- **The Clash.** One owed Clash goes in each burst: an identification payload with the station's own id (item 1) and the Clash (item 2). It has its own request with the access rules in the ordinary class (`_clashRequest`), built when granted. It raises `ModemSent` with the new `ModemSentKind.Clash`, does not call `OnIdentificationBurstStarted` or `OnOtherTransmissionEnded`, so the ten-minute clock, `IdentificationDue` and the Morse ident are untouched. A Clash decoded for a number cancels the one owed for it, whether still owed or built and waiting (`HeardClash`); one naming this station's number and id moves it.
- **Test seams.** Internal constructors fix the first number and id (taken at `SetIdentity`, so a station still has none before it); the rigs use them with the numbers 1, 2, 3 and an id made from the number, so every existing scenario keeps its flow. `HearHeaderFrom` and `HearNumber` stand for a header read and a heard sender without audio, and `IdentificationPayloadBuilt` hands a test the payload of each identification burst built. `SimRig` takes `numbers`, `ids` and `randomNumbers`.

Figures that moved: the identification burst is longer, by its station id, which is 6 bytes (4 symbols, 0.18 s at the first-contact format), so the three clock tests that look for an identification burst by its length compute it with the id. Nothing else in an existing scenario moved. The tests of a station with no identity had a premise that is gone (a burst for "number 2" reached a station that had no identity): `A_Listener_Delivers_Measures_And_Runs_The_Rules_As_Usual...` is now `A_Listener_Has_No_Number_Is_Never_Addressed_Measures_And_Learns...`, and the station-identity test that gave an identity after hearing a burst addressed to it no longer expects to have received it.

Choices the design left open, taken as the simplest that fits it:

1. The ten minutes are `LinkAdaptation.ForgetSeconds`, the time a neighbour is forgotten after, in both places the design uses them (a number counts as heard; two ids "inside ten minutes").
2. A station moving takes a number not heard in the ten minutes, never the one it is leaving, even when it has heard all 63.
3. A station that is transmitting or in its own mute tail does not move on reading its own number. It will on the next burst.
4. A Clash is one item to a burst; several owed go one after another. One is owed for a number at a time, a newer id replacing an older one.
5. A station that is named in a Clash while its Clash for the same number is owed or built waiting drops its own (it is told).
6. A station with no identity forgets a changed number and owes no Clash.
7. The data a station had queued for a forgotten address is dropped (the brief said so), where an address forgotten for silence still sends its datagrams on to everyone.
8. A burst built before a move is discarded and built again, not patched.

Found, recorded and not worked round:

- **A lost Clash is not repeated.** Two stations that both owe a Clash for the same number can send together (they cannot hear each other in time), and then neither is decoded. The clash stays until the other station identifies; the third stations have the later id on record, so the other station's next identification looks like a change and the Clash then names it.
- **The survivor is told to move at its next identification.** After a Clash the station that stayed keeps its number, but the stations that heard the Clash still hold the other's id as the last one heard on it. The survivor's next identification, which comes at least ten minutes after its last and so always inside ten minutes of the moved station's, is a different id on that number, and a Clash names it too: both stations move, ten minutes apart. A test shows it (`A_Station_Left_On_A_Number_After_A_Clash_Is_Told_To_Move_At_Its_Next_Identification`).
- A station that reads its own number can only be found when it is not transmitting; in the simulated channel two stations in hearing of each other with one number do not notice each other at once (the first to be heard is the only one that is), so "both notice" happens in the tests with a third station that both hear.
- Clashes between bursts that carry no id (a hidden pair that sends only data for under ten minutes) are not found until one identifies, as the design says, and in the meantime a third station's table of addresses at the number takes whichever burst it heard last.

## The sudden judgement, as fixed

27 November 2026, packet-net/pdn-soundmodem#898. The receiver judged some clean first-contact bursts sudden, and so took no measurement from them, depending only on what bytes they held; and on clean paths it missed collisions at the dense end. What was found, what changed and what it measured. The library changed in one place (`PayloadReceiver.IsSudden`); the public API is as it was.

**What was found.** A symbol's miss is its mean squared distance from its constellation points, in units of the noise measured on the opening. Where the noise sets it, the miss is about 2 and moves by about 15 percent from symbol to symbol at first contact, less at the wider formats (a symbol's miss averages its carriers: 56 at first contact, 120 at format 140, 162 at 124). On a quiet path the noise does not set it. The sender holds every sample within its peak limit, and at first contact it drives 6 dB over full drive into 56 carriers under 1.5 kHz: about 80 samples of every symbol are clipped, and the share of that distortion that lands on the burst's own carriers varies from under a tenth of its average to three times it or more, set by the symbol's bits. From about 25 dB over the format's threshold the distortion is larger than the noise, and a symbol's miss measures how hard the limit hit it: 0.5 to 10 within one clean burst, as step 8 saw. The same bursts made without the limit, and with no noise at all, miss about 1 to 4. The opening is clipped too, differently, so its median (1.3 to 1.7 on a quiet path) is no yardstick for the payload. The prototype's rule needed the earlier part's median under 1.5 times the opening's plus 0.5, and the later part's median over three times the earlier part's. On a quiet path a clean burst whose later symbols happened to be clipped harder passed both. At format 124 a collision on a clean path also raised the earlier symbols' miss, through the receiver's tracking of the whole burst, so the earlier part failed "clean" and the collision was missed. The miss itself is right for noise: what the opening cannot show is how hard the limit hit each payload symbol, which only rebuilding every symbol through the limit would give. So the judgement was changed, not the miss.

**The rule now.** Sudden when some point splits the payload into an earlier part of at least two symbols and a later part of at least three, and the later part's median miss is over three times the largest of: the earlier part's worst symbol, the opening's median miss, and 0.5. A payload under six symbols is never sudden. A clean burst's symbols scatter, but its later ones are not all three times worse than the worst of its earlier ones; another burst coming on makes them so. It allocates nothing. Other rules tried, on the same bursts: the same comparison without the opening, or with a later part of two symbols, still judged a few clean bursts sudden (one wild last symbol is enough for a part of two). Two rules that keep the old one's reach for weak interferers at the dense end, the later part's median over three times the earlier part's and over every symbol before it (opening included), and over three times the earlier part's and five standard errors of the burst's own symbol-to-symbol scatter, judged 19 and 6 of about 27 000 clean bursts sudden, nearly all at first contact 30 dB or more over the threshold.

**What it costs.** Reach for an interferer 20 dB under the burst. At a dense format on a path near its threshold such an interferer fails the burst while raising the miss only about threefold, the most a dense constellation's miss shows, and the new rule catches fewer of those (format 124, a path 10 dB over its threshold: 25, 15 and 10 of 50 at a quarter, a half and three quarters, against 44, 47 and 48; 55 of the 150 decoded all the same). The rest read as the path failing. Step 8's interferer 4 dB weaker under capture is the same case. At the robust formats on quiet paths such bursts decode, and are now measured with the interferer in them where before most were not.

**Measured.** `SuddenJudgementTests` (gated, `SM_MASK_GATE=1`, about 15 000 bursts, seven minutes on the build box): seeded random datagrams of 8 to 48 symbols, white noise at a per-carrier signal-to-noise ratio over each format's threshold (the ratio at which half of 32 bursts decode, measured by the test), and "quiet", the sims' own link (gain 1, FM-shaped noise of 0.004 rms). ## Decided (Tom, 26 November 2026)

1. The shape is approved and is to be built in the order above: the station's only clock is the count of audio samples received, the library has no threads of its own, and the host interface is defined here, in the library, so that any program can use the modem.
2. The identification burst's payload is the callsign as plain text behind a length byte, then the 16-bit capability word. It goes into the protocol document with step 7.

## Decided (Tom, 27 November 2026)

1. The transmitter identifier callsign is given by the host through the host interface (`SetIdentity`), and the station does not transmit until it has one. A different one is refused. It is not an address: several stations may use the same one, and it is distinct from the names a host sends to and from.

2. No closing identification ("we don't need a closing identification"): an idle station never keys to identify. An identification is due when a transmission of the station's ends and either it has never identified or the interval (ten minutes of the sample clock) has passed since its last identification burst started; nothing else makes one due, and there is no identification timer. Transmissions within the interval of an identification are covered by it. The Morse ident in pdn-soundmodem is being changed the same way. This supersedes the case in "Step 7 as built" of a station that identified, transmitted again and went quiet.


## Decided (Tom, 27 November 2026, link addresses)

The modem is a link layer with addresses of its own, as a LAN is: each datagram has a link address it is for and one it is from, the modem learns where an address lives from the "from" of every burst it hears, and station numbers become the modem's own business. The agreed design is docs/station-numbers.md ("so effectively MAC addresses and ARP. OK"). It is built in two steps: step one, link addresses and learning, is built and described in "Link addresses as built"; step two (random station numbers, the station id, Clash, a number changing hands) is not.

## Choices this design makes

Settled without being put to Tom, because each is a starting value or an internal matter. Each is a fixed value in the code, not a setting, and the bench may move it.

1. **First slot and random delay:** the prototype's draw, 0.8 s plus up to 0.4 s. The protocol sets no figure.
2. **Counting a silence:** a fixed 3 s after the burst's end, to be reset once the decode time of this implementation is measured.
3. **Decode work is run by the caller.** No threads in the library, and tests are deterministic.
4. **A candidate whose header will not read** holds the channel busy for the opening plus one second, then is judged again.
5. **One burst's length:** a ceiling of airtime (the prototype's 2.5 s to start) or one datagram, whichever is the longer, so that a datagram that needs a long burst still goes whole.
6. **`Choose` is called only once the channel is free**, and the burst is rendered at once, so there is nothing to undo when a request is withdrawn.
7. **Transmitter duty limits belong to the audio layer**, which knows the transmitter.
